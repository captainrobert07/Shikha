// What I do

Six hats,
one person.

On any given day, the work moves between engineering, modelling, storytelling, governance, leadership, and the quiet, careful art of keeping things working.

i.

I build the pipes.

Data engineering is plumbing with opinions. Ingestion from a dozen source systems, transformations that survive a re-run, orchestration that nobody has to babysit, lineage you can actually trace.

ii.

I shape the model.

The semantic layer is where the business meets the data. Clean fact and dimension tables, careful slowly-changing logic, DAX that performs, security that doesn't quietly leak.

iii.

I tell the story.

Reports nobody asks twice about. Dashboards designed for the decision, not the demo. The ten-second answer for the executive and the ten-page answer for the analyst, on the same page.

iv.

I keep it honest.

Catalogue, lineage, classification, quality rules, stewardship — the unsexy infrastructure that keeps regulated data trustworthy and audit-ready.

v.

I lead the team.

Deputy Manager means the people are part of the product. Hiring with care, mentoring with patience, defending focus, making sure good work is seen.

vi.

I make it boring.

The highest compliment a dashboard gets is silence on Monday morning. Predictable, reproducible, well-documented work — so the team can spend its weekends actually resting.

// How I work

Four things
I believe.

i.

Start with the question, not the data.

Every good dashboard begins with someone trying to make a decision. If I can't write that decision down in one sentence, I'm not ready to build anything yet.

ii.

If it needs explaining, it isn't done.

A report that comes with a footnote, a caveat, and a 20-minute walkthrough is just an unfinished report. The work of clarity happens before the share button, not after.

iii.

Default to teaching.

Every shortcut I take alone is a shortcut my team can't take tomorrow. Documentation, code reviews, pair sessions — they cost time once and save it forever.

iv.

Boring is a feature.

Production data work shouldn't be exciting. Surprises mean somebody is about to have a long Monday. Predictable, reproducible, well-documented — and we save the excitement for the discovery work.
