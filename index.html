// Why I do this

Most of what gets called data is just numbers waiting for a translator. I spend my days making sure the right ones reach the right person at the right time — usually before someone has to ask.

That means a lot of unglamorous work that nobody sees: pipelines that don't break overnight, models that hold up under questioning, dashboards that are quietly accurate on a Monday morning, and a team that has the autonomy and the standards to keep it all standing up. Most days, the kindest thing I can deliver is a boring dashboard — because boring means trusted.

// What I do

SIX HATS,
ONE PERSON.

On any given day, the work moves between engineering, modelling, storytelling, governance, leadership, and the quiet, careful art of keeping things working.
i.

I BUILD
THE PIPES

Data engineering is plumbing with opinions. Ingestion from a dozen source systems, transformations that survive a re-run, orchestration that nobody has to baby-sit, and lineage you can actually trace. Mostly Microsoft Fabric and Azure these days; SQL all the way down.

ii.

I SHAPE
THE MODEL

The semantic layer is where the business meets the data. Clean fact and dimension tables, careful slowly-changing logic, DAX that performs, row-level security that doesn't quietly leak. The bit a good viz can't fake.

iii.

I TELL
THE STORY

Reports nobody asked twice about. Dashboards designed for the decision, not the demo. The ten-second answer for the executive and the ten-page answer for the analyst, on the same page.

iv.

I KEEP
IT HONEST

Catalogue, lineage, classification, quality rules, stewardship. The unsexy infrastructure that keeps regulated data trustworthy and audit-ready. Informatica CDGC certified — and a believer.

v.

I LEAD
THE TEAM

Deputy Manager means the people are part of the product. Hiring with care, mentoring with patience, defending focus, and making sure good work is seen. I lead by listening, not by volume.

vi.

I MAKE IT
BORING

The highest compliment a dashboard gets is silence on Monday morning. Predictable, reproducible, well-documented work — so the team can spend its weekends actually resting.

// How I work

FOUR THINGS
I BELIEVE.

START WITH THE QUESTION,
NOT THE DATA.

Every good dashboard begins with someone trying to make a decision. If I can't write that decision down in one sentence, I'm not ready to build anything yet.

IF IT NEEDS
EXPLAINING, IT'S NOT DONE.

A report that comes with a footnote, a caveat, and a 20-minute walkthrough is just an unfinished report. The work of clarity happens before the share button, not after.

DEFAULT TO TEACHING.

Every shortcut I take alone is a shortcut my team can't take tomorrow. Documentation, code reviews, pair sessions — they cost time once and save it forever.

BORING IS
A FEATURE.

Production data work shouldn't be exciting. Surprises mean somebody is about to have a long Monday. I aim for predictable, reproducible, well-documented — and I save the excitement for the discovery work.
