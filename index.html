02 — About

A translator for the numbers behind the decisions.

Most of what gets called data is just numbers waiting for a translator. I spend my days making sure the right ones reach the right person at the right time — usually before someone has to ask.

That means a lot of unglamorous work nobody sees: pipelines that don't break overnight, models that hold up under questioning, dashboards that are quietly accurate on a Monday morning, and a team with the autonomy and the standards to keep it all standing up. The kindest thing I can ship most days is a boring dashboard — because boring means trusted.

06 — Method

Four things I believe.

i.

Start with the question, not the data.

Every good dashboard begins with someone trying to make a decision. If I can't write that decision down in one sentence, I'm not ready to build anything yet.

ii.

If it needs explaining, it isn't done.

A report that comes with a footnote, a caveat, and a 20-minute walkthrough is just an unfinished report. The work of clarity happens before the share button, not after.

iii.

Default to teaching.

Every shortcut I take alone is one my team can't take tomorrow. Documentation, code reviews, pair sessions — they cost time once and save it forever.

iv.

Boring is a feature.

Production data work shouldn't be exciting. Surprises mean somebody's about to have a long Monday. Predictable, reproducible, well-documented — and we save the excitement for the discovery work.
